\begin{abstract}
We prove the validity statements of CORD for the cross-fitted, learned scores of the
implementation: super-uniform conformal p-values, false discovery rate (FDR) control of fold-wise
BH, FDR control of e-BH on pooled conformal e-values under arbitrary dependence between folds,
the selection floor, the exact null distribution of the presence test, and the bound
$\mathrm{FDR}\le\alpha/(1-\varepsilon)$ under a share $\varepsilon$ of poisoned calibration points
placed arbitrarily (Proposition~4, for audits that do not depend on clean calibration scores). The proofs are
self-contained. They rest on one structural lemma (conditionally on everything except which clean
points went to the fold and which to the calibration set, all scores are fixed and that split is
uniformly random) and on a classical backward supermartingale. Numbering follows proposal v3.3.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting and notation}

The untrusted pool is a finite set of labelled samples, some of them modified by the attacker
(\emph{poisoned}); a sample is \emph{clean} if the attacker did not modify it. The defender draws a
random hold-out $\C=\Ctr\cup\Cal$ and partitions the rest into folds $\D_1,\dots,\D_J$. Fold model
$f_{-j}$ is trained on $\D\setminus\D_j$ only. For fold $j$ the \emph{scored set} is
$S_j=\D_j\cup\C$; every point of $S_j$ receives a fused score $s_j(\cdot)$, larger meaning more
suspicious.

The calibration audit (Section~3.3 of the proposal) removes points of $\Cal$; we write $\Cal$ for
the calibration set \emph{after} the audit and $m=|\Cal|$. Within fold $j$: $N_j=|\D_j|$, $\Hnull$
the set of clean points, $N_j^0=|\Hnull\cap\D_j|$, $n=\sum_j N_j$. For a threshold $t$,
\begin{gather*}
V_j(t)=\#\{c\in\Cal: s_j(c)\ge t\},\qquad K_j(t)=\#\{i\in\D_j: s_j(i)\ge t\},\\
K^0_j(t)=\#\{i\in\D_j\cap\Hnull: s_j(i)\ge t\}.
\end{gather*}
Conformal p-value, BH stopping threshold and conformal e-value (proposal eqs.~(2)--(3), with the
correction of Remark~\ref{rem:threshold}):
\begin{gather*}
p_i=\frac{1+V_j(s_j(i))}{1+m},\qquad
e_i=\frac{(1+m)\,\1\{s_j(i)\ge T_j\}}{1+V_j(T_j)},\\
T_j=\min\Bigl\{t\in\Sigma_j:\ K_j(t)\ge1,\ \frac{(1+V_j(t))\,N_j}{(1+m)\,K_j(t)}\le\alpha_{\mathrm{bh}}\Bigr\},
\end{gather*}
for $i\in\D_j$, where $\Sigma_j=\{s_j(z):z\in\D_j\cup\Cal\}$ is the set of \emph{pooled} fold and
calibration scores, $\min\emptyset=+\infty$ and $e_i=0$ when $T_j=+\infty$. The set
$\{i\in\D_j:s_j(i)\ge T_j\}$ is the BH rejection set at level $\alpha_{\mathrm{bh}}$: if $s^\ast$ is the
smallest rejected fold score, no fold score lies in $[T_j,s^\ast)$, because the BH condition, once
it holds at some $t$, keeps holding up to the next fold score ($K_j$ is constant there and $V_j$
non-increasing). Code:
\texttt{cord.stats.conformal}, \texttt{cord.pipeline.run\_cord}; the implementation agrees with
the reference code of Bashari et al.\ (\texttt{tests/test\_reference\_evalues.py}).

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[A1, clean exchangeability]\label{as:A1}
Let $\G_j$ be the $\sigma$-field generated by: the samples of $\D\setminus\D_j$ (features and
given labels); the poisoned samples of $S_j$ together with the part ($\D_j$, $\Ctr$ or the
pre-audit calibration part) each belongs to; the samples of $\Ctr$; the unordered set
$\Q_j=(\D_j\cup\Cal)\cap\Hnull$ of clean points of the fold and of the calibration set; and all
randomness of training and scoring that is independent of the data (seeds). Conditionally on
$\G_j$, the subset of $\Q_j$ that lies in $\D_j$ is uniformly distributed over the subsets of
$\Q_j$ of size $N_j^0$.
\end{assumption}

In mode~T1, A1 follows from the construction: the hold-out and the folds are a uniformly random
partition of the pool drawn after poisoning (proposal Section~3.2), independently of the data, so
once everything except the split of $\Q_j$ is fixed, that split is uniform. This holds whatever
rule the attacker used to choose which samples to poison (classes C1--C3 of Table~2). A1 also
requires the calibration audit to remove only poisoned points, so that every clean calibration
point is kept; this is the ``uncontaminated $\Cal$'' hypothesis of Proposition~1.

\begin{assumption}[A2, score invariance]\label{as:A2}
For each fold $j$ there is a $\G_j$-measurable function $\varphi_j$ such that
$s_j(z)=\varphi_j(z)$ for every $z\in S_j$. That every score is a function of the scored
point and of quantities that are either independent of $(\D_j,\Cal)$ or invariant to permutations
of $\D_j\cup\Cal$ is a sufficient condition.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[A3, no ties]\label{as:A3}
Almost surely, the scores $\{s_j(z):z\in\D_j\cup\Cal\}$ are pairwise distinct.
\end{assumption}

A3 is used in Proposition~1(ii)--(iii) and Proposition~4(b)--(c); Proposition~1(i) and
Proposition~3 hold without it (Remarks~\ref{rem:ties-p} and~\ref{rem:ties-pres}). \emph{The
implementation enforces A3}: before calibration it replaces the scores of $\D_j\cup\Cal$ by their
pooled ranks with ties broken by independent uniform variables (\texttt{cord.pipeline.random\_tiebreak}).
The uniforms are independent of the data and of the split, so A1--A2 are kept; and every procedure
below depends on the scores only through their order, so nothing changes when scores are already
distinct.

\section{Two structural lemmas}

\begin{lemma}[Conditional structure]\label{lem:structure}
Under A1 and A2, conditionally on $\G_j$: (a) the score $s_j(z)$ of every $z\in S_j$ is fixed;
(b) the split of $\Q_j$ into $\D_j\cap\Hnull$ (size $N_j^0$) and $\Cal$ (size $m$) is uniformly
random; (c) the scores of the poisoned points of $\D_j$ are fixed, and so are their labels as
members of $\D_j$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) is A2: $s_j=\varphi_j$ with $\varphi_j$ fixed given $\G_j$, and each point's features and
given label are fixed given $\G_j$ (the points of $\Q_j$ are fixed as a set; only their part is
random). (b) is A1. (c) holds because the poisoned points of $S_j$ and their parts are
$\G_j$-measurable.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:structure} reduces every statement below to a combinatorial one: a fixed list of
$N_j^0+m$ distinct numbers (the clean scores), a uniformly random choice of which $N_j^0$ of them
are ``fold'' and which $m$ are ``calibration'', and a fixed list of non-null fold scores.

\begin{lemma}[The implementation satisfies A2]\label{lem:A2}
Each component of the CORD pipeline, as implemented, satisfies A2:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Fold model.} $f_{-j}$ is trained on $\D\setminus\D_j$ with data-independent seeds
  (\texttt{Split.train\_indices}); it is $\G_j$-measurable. The hold-out is never trained on.
\item \emph{Training-time quantities.} The confidence trajectory is the fold model's checkpoint
  predictions on the scored points; checkpoints are functions of $\D\setminus\D_j$ and the seeds.
\item \emph{Perturbation signals.} SCALE-UP predictions are functions of the point and of
  $f_{-j}$. STRIP blends each point with fixed partners drawn from $\D\setminus\D_j$, which
  are $\G_j$-measurable.
\item \emph{Class statistics and neighbour graphs.} Latent-geometry statistics (robust class means
  and covariances, PCA, spectral directions), the class-median gradient direction and the
  $k$-nearest-neighbour graph of the label-content signal are computed on the whole scored set
  $S_j$ (the point itself excluded for neighbours). As functions of the \emph{unordered} set $S_j$
  they are invariant to permutations of $\D_j\cup\Cal$; and $S_j$, as an unordered set, is
  $\G_j$-measurable. Singular vectors are defined only up to sign, and a numerical SVD may pick
  the sign by row order; the signals use only sign-invariant functions of them (squared
  projections, Mahalanobis distances after PCA).
\item \emph{Frozen encoder.} A public pretrained encoder does not depend on the pool.
\item \emph{Rank normalisation and naive fusions.} Within-set ranks over $S_j$, their mean and
  maximum are symmetric functions of $S_j$.
\item \emph{Audit of $\Ctr$ (new in v3.2).} The points of $\Ctr$ to inspect are the top
  $b_{\mathrm{tr}}$ of $\Ctr$ under the mean of within-set ranks (a symmetric function of $S_j$,
  item~(vi)); the points removed are those inspected and poisoned. Both depend on $\D_j\cup\Cal$
  only through $S_j$ as a set, and on the poison identities of $\Ctr$, which are in $\G_j$.
\item \emph{Positive-unlabeled fusion.} The classifier is fitted on the rows of $S_j$ with
  label~0 for the (audited) $\Ctr$ and label~1 for $\D_j\cup\Cal$; the fitting algorithm with a
  fixed seed is a function of its training \emph{multiset}. Its output is therefore a fixed
  function $g_j$ given $\G_j$, and the fused score is $g_j$ applied to the point's signal vector.
\item \emph{Tie-breaking of the fused score (new in v3.2).} The implementation orders points by
  $g_j$ and breaks ties by the mean rank of item~(vi), a symmetric function of $S_j$.
\item \emph{Shadow-model agreement (proposal v3.3, Table~4; not yet implemented).} A shadow model
  that keeps the backdoor and erases clean behaviour, after RBD [16], satisfies A2 if it is derived
  from $f_{-j}$ using only data outside $\D_j\cup\Cal$ (e.g.\ $\D\setminus\D_j$ or the audited
  $\Ctr$) and data-independent seeds: it is then $\G_j$-measurable, like item~(i). Fitting it on
  points of $\D_j\cup\Cal$ would break A2 unless the fit is a symmetric function of that set.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Items (i)--(iii), (v) and (x) are functions of the point and of $\G_j$-measurable objects. Items
(iv), (vi), (vii) and (ix) are functions of the point and of symmetric functions of $S_j$
(together with $\G_j$-measurable labels), and a symmetric function of $S_j$ is fixed given $\G_j$
because $S_j=\Q_j\cup\{\text{poisoned points of }S_j\}\cup\Ctr$ as an unordered set. For (viii),
the fit depends on $\D_j\cup\Cal$ only through the multiset of its rows, which is fixed given
$\G_j$. Composition of such maps preserves the property.
\end{proof}

order-invariance of the histogram gradient-boosting fit (exact up to floating-point summation
order; the test suite checks equivariance to $10^{-5}$) and of logistic regression. A statistician should decide whether to state (viii) for an idealised exact fit.}

\section{A backward supermartingale}

\begin{lemma}[Optional stopping]\label{lem:martingale}
Let $v_{(1)}<\dots<v_{(r)}$ be fixed distinct numbers, $r=N^0+m$, and assign the label ``fold'' to
a uniformly random subset of $N^0$ of them and ``calibration'' to the other $m$. Reveal the
numbers from the smallest upwards: after $k$ steps, $\F_k$ is generated by the labels of
$v_{(1)},\dots,v_{(k)}$. Let $X_k$ and $Y_k$ be the numbers of fold and calibration labels among
$v_{(k+1)},\dots,v_{(r)}$, and $M_k=X_k/(1+Y_k)$. Then $(M_k)_{k=0}^{r}$ is an
$(\F_k)$-supermartingale, and for every $(\F_k)$-stopping time $\tau\le r$,
\[
\E[M_\tau]\le M_0=\frac{N^0}{1+m}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Given $\F_k$, the remaining $X_k+Y_k=r-k$ labels are a uniformly random arrangement of $X_k$
fold and $Y_k$ calibration labels, so $v_{(k+1)}$ is a fold point with probability
$X_k/(r-k)$. If $Y_k\ge1$,
\[
\E[M_{k+1}\mid\F_k]=\frac{X_k}{r-k}\cdot\frac{X_k-1}{1+Y_k}+\frac{Y_k}{r-k}\cdot\frac{X_k}{Y_k}
=\frac{X_k}{r-k}\cdot\frac{X_k-1+1+Y_k}{1+Y_k}=\frac{X_k}{1+Y_k}=M_k .
\]
If $Y_k=0$, the next point is a fold point and $M_{k+1}=X_k-1\le M_k$ (and $M$ stays at $0$ once
$X_k=0$). Hence $M$ is a supermartingale; $\tau$ is bounded, so optional stopping gives
$\E[M_\tau]\le\E[M_0]$, and $M_0=N^0/(1+m)$ is deterministic.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Upward-scanning thresholds]\label{lem:stopping}
Assume A1--A3 and work conditionally on $\G_j$. Allow $\Cal$ to contain poisoned points, provided
the set $B$ of them is $\G_j$-measurable; write $V_j=V^0_j+V^B_j$ (clean and poisoned calibration
counts) and $m^0=m-|B|$; for an uncontaminated $\Cal$, $V^0_j=V_j$ and $m^0=m$. Let $T$ be a
threshold chosen by scanning the
pooled scores $\Sigma_j$ (and, if desired, other fixed values) in increasing order and stopping at
the first $t$ for which a rule depending only on $\bigl(t, V_j(t'),K_j(t')\bigr)_{t'\le t}$ holds
($T=+\infty$ if none does). Candidate values must not depend on the fold/calibration labels of
clean points above $t$; in particular, ``the smallest \emph{fold} score at which the rule holds''
is \emph{not} of this form (Remark~\ref{rem:threshold}). Then
\[
\E\!\left[\frac{K^0_j(T)}{1+V^0_j(T)}\,\middle|\,\G_j\right]\le\frac{N_j^0}{1+m^0}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:martingale} to the clean scores $\{s_j(z):z\in\Q_j\}$, which are fixed and
distinct with a uniformly random fold/calibration split (Lemma~\ref{lem:structure}, A3). For $t$
in $(v_{(k)},v_{(k+1)}]$ the clean points with score $\ge t$ are exactly
$v_{(k+1)},\dots,v_{(r)}$, so $V^0_j(t)=Y_k$ and $K^0_j(t)=X_k$, while
$V_j(t)=Y_k+V^B_j(t)$ and $K_j(t)=X_k+K^1_j(t)$, where $V^B_j(t)$ and $K^1_j(t)$ count the poisoned
calibration and fold points with score $\ge t$, fixed functions of $t$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:structure}(c)
and $B\in\G_j$). Here Lemma~\ref{lem:martingale} is applied with $m^0$ calibration labels. Hence whether the scan has stopped at some
$t'\le t$ is $\F_k$-measurable for $t\in(v_{(k)},v_{(k+1)}]$, and $\tau=\#\{\text{clean scores}<T\}$
(with $\tau=r$ if $T=+\infty$) is an $(\F_k)$-stopping time. Then
$K^0_j(T)/(1+V^0_j(T))=M_\tau$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:martingale} applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Null mass of conformal e-values]\label{lem:evalues}
Under A1--A3 and an uncontaminated $\Cal$, for every fold $j$,
\[
\textstyle\sum_{i\in\D_j\cap\Hnull}\E[e_i]\le N_j^0 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$T_j$ is the first pooled score, scanning upwards, at which $K_j(t)\ge1$ and
$(1+V_j(t))N_j\le\alpha_{\mathrm{bh}}(1+m)K_j(t)$, a rule of the form in Lemma~\ref{lem:stopping}. Every clean fold point with $s_j(i)\ge T_j$ has
$e_i=(1+m)/(1+V_j(T_j))$, so $\sum_{i\in\D_j\cap\Hnull}e_i=(1+m)K^0_j(T_j)/(1+V_j(T_j))$. Take
expectations with Lemma~\ref{lem:stopping} and then the expectation over $\G_j$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Which threshold, and a correction to proposal eq.~(3)]\label{rem:threshold}
Lemma~\ref{lem:evalues} needs $T_j$ to be a stopping value of the upward scan over the
\emph{pooled} scores; it may be a calibration score. Proposal v3.1 defined $T_j$ as the smallest
score among the BH rejections, a fold score $s^\ast\ge T_j$. That gives the same selections but
counts $V_j(s^\ast)\le V_j(T_j)$, hence larger e-values, and the decision to stop at $s^\ast$ is not a
stopping time: whether the next point up is a fold point is not yet revealed. The bound then
fails: with $N_j^0=5$ clean and $40$ strongly shifted fold points, $m=2$ and
$\alpha_{\mathrm{bh}}=0.9$, the null mass $\sum_{i}\E[e_i]$ was $1.22\,N_j^0$ in simulation
($40{,}000$ repetitions, standard error $0.003\,N_j^0$), against $1.00\,N_j^0$ with the stopping
value. The implementation uses the stopping value (\texttt{cord.stats.conformal\_evalues};
regression test in \texttt{test\_reference\_evalues.py}).

The reference code of Bashari et al.\ stops its scan with the estimate $V N_j/(mK)$ instead of
BH's $(1+V)N_j/((1+m)K)$. Both are stopping values of the same scan, hence both give valid
e-values; CORD's is never lower, so it never selects more.
\end{remark}

\section{Proposition 1: validity of the three outputs}

\begin{proposition}[Prop.~1]\label{prop:validity}
Assume A1, A2 and an uncontaminated $\Cal$ (the audit removed every poisoned calibration point
and no clean one). Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item for every clean $i\in\D_j$ and $t\in[0,1]$, $\Pr(p_i\le t)\le t$; hence the expected share of
  clean points in the removal set $R=\{i:p_i\le t\}$ is at most $t$;
\item under A3, BH at level $\alpha$ within fold $j$ has
  $\mathrm{FDR}\le\alpha\,\E[N_j^0]/N_j\le\alpha$;
\item under A3, e-BH at level $\alpha$ applied to the pooled e-values $(e_i)_{i\in\D}$ has
  $\mathrm{FDR}\le\alpha\,\E|\Hnull\cap\D|/n\le\alpha$, whatever the dependence between folds.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Fix a clean $i\in\D_j$ and condition on $\G_j$ and on the set $W=\Cal\cup\{i\}$ (unordered),
together with the fold/calibration labels of $\Q_j\setminus W$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:structure}(b),
$i$ is then uniformly distributed over the $m+1$ points of $W$, whose scores are fixed. Under A3,
$p_i=(1+\#\{c\in W\setminus\{i\}:s_j(c)\ge s_j(i)\})/(1+m)$ is the rank of $i$ in $W$ from the top
divided by $m+1$, uniform on $\{1/(m+1),\dots,1\}$, so $\Pr(p_i\le t)=\lfloor t(m+1)\rfloor/(m+1)\le t$.
Without A3 see Remark~\ref{rem:ties-p}. For the clean share of $R$, let $H=|\Hnull\cap\D|$,
which is $\G_j$-measurable for every $j$. Then
\[
\E\Bigl[\frac{|R\cap\Hnull|}{H}\Bigr]=\sum_j\E\Bigl[\frac{1}{H}\,\E\Bigl[\textstyle\sum_{i\in\D_j\cap\Hnull}\1\{p_i\le t\}\Bigm|\G_j\Bigr]\Bigr]
\le\sum_j\E\Bigl[\frac{tN_j^0}{H}\Bigr]=t .
\]

(ii) Let $T_\alpha$ be the stopping value $T_j$ with $\alpha_{\mathrm{bh}}=\alpha$. The BH rejections
are the fold points with score $\ge T_\alpha$, and $K_j(T_\alpha)\ge1$ when $T_\alpha<\infty$. At
$T_\alpha$ the BH condition gives
$1/K_j(T_\alpha)\le\alpha(1+m)/\bigl(N_j(1+V_j(T_\alpha))\bigr)$. Hence
\[
\mathrm{FDP}=\frac{K^0_j(T_\alpha)}{K_j(T_\alpha)\vee1}\le\frac{\alpha(1+m)}{N_j}\cdot\frac{K^0_j(T_\alpha)}{1+V_j(T_\alpha)},
\]
and Lemma~\ref{lem:stopping} gives
$\E[\mathrm{FDP}\mid\G_j]\le\frac{\alpha(1+m)}{N_j}\cdot\frac{N_j^0}{1+m}=\alpha N_j^0/N_j$; take
expectations over $\G_j$.

(iii) e-BH selects $R=\{i:e_i\ge n/(\alpha k^\ast)\}$ with
$k^\ast=\max\{k:e_{(k)}\ge n/(\alpha k)\}$ and $|R|=k^\ast$ (or $R=\emptyset$). For every $i$,
$\1\{i\in R\}/(|R|\vee1)\le\alpha e_i/n$. Summing over null hypotheses and using
Lemma~\ref{lem:evalues} fold by fold,
\[
\mathrm{FDR}=\E\sum_{i\in\Hnull\cap\D}\frac{\1\{i\in R\}}{|R|\vee1}
\le\frac{\alpha}{n}\sum_{j}\E\Bigl[\textstyle\sum_{i\in\D_j\cap\Hnull}e_i\Bigr]\le\frac{\alpha}{n}\sum_j \E[N_j^0]
=\frac{\alpha\,\E|\Hnull\cap\D|}{n}.
\]
Only expectations of single e-values enter, so no assumption on the dependence between folds is
needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Ties in (i)]\label{rem:ties-p}
With ties, break them by independent uniform variables $U$: the resulting $\tilde p_i$ is exactly
uniform on the grid by the argument above, and the implemented $p_i$, which counts calibration
ties against the test point, satisfies $p_i\ge\tilde p_i$. Hence $\Pr(p_i\le t)\le t$ still holds.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What the proof needs from the attacker]
Nothing beyond A1: the proof conditions on the poisoned points, so their number, location, scores
and the rule that chose them are arbitrary. Validity is a statement about clean points only; it
bounds false alarms and never missed poison.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Ties in (ii)--(iii)]
Resolved by construction: the implementation breaks ties at random before calibration
(Section~2, after A3), which matters for discrete scores such as SCALE-UP consistency and for
residual ties of the fused score. \texttt{tests/test\_stats.py} checks FDR control on heavily tied
discrete scores.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Pre-specification]\label{rem:prespec}
The guarantees hold for a procedure fixed before the evaluated data are seen: $\alpha$,
$\alpha_{\mathrm{bh}}$, $t$, $k$, the signal set, the fusion method and the audit budgets. Choosing
any of them on the evaluated data (e.g.\ the $\alpha_{\mathrm{bh}}$ sweep of Section~6.1, or picking the
best of the $B$ hold-out redraws) voids them. The pilot's conditions are development data; headline
results must use attacks, seeds and datasets not used for these choices (proposal Section~4.1).
Averaging the FDP over hold-out redraws estimates the FDR of one redraw; it is not a different
procedure.
\end{remark}

\section{Proposition 2: selection floor}

\begin{proposition}[Prop.~2]\label{prop:floor}
(i) Every conformal p-value is at least $1/(m+1)$, and BH at level $\alpha$ on $N$ hypotheses
rejects $k\ge1$ of them only if $1/(m+1)\le\alpha k/N$. (ii) Every conformal e-value is at most
$m+1$, and e-BH at level $\alpha$ on $n$ hypotheses selects $k\ge1$ of them only if
$m+1\ge n/(\alpha k)$. In both cases a non-empty selection has selected fraction
\[
\frac{k}{N}\ \ge\ \rho_{\mathrm{sel}}=\frac{1}{\alpha(m+1)} .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) $p_i\ge1/(m+1)$ because the count in the numerator is at least one. BH rejects $k$ hypotheses
only if $p_{(k)}\le\alpha k/N$, hence only if $1/(m+1)\le\alpha k/N$. (ii) $e_i\le(1+m)/(1+V_j(T_j))
\le1+m$. e-BH selects $k$ only if $e_{(k)}\ge n/(\alpha k)$, hence only if $m+1\ge n/(\alpha k)$.
Both rearrange to $k/N\ge1/(\alpha(m+1))$ (with $N=n$ in (ii)).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Below the floor]
If the poisoning rate is $\rho<\rho_{\mathrm{sel}}$, every non-empty selection has
$\mathrm{FDP}\ge1-\rho/\rho_{\mathrm{sel}}$, because it holds at least $\rho_{\mathrm{sel}}N$ points of
which at most $\rho N$ are poisoned. Under FDR control at level $\alpha$ (Proposition~1),
\[
\Pr(\text{any selection})\le\frac{\alpha}{1-\rho/\rho_{\mathrm{sel}}}.
\]
So below the floor a selection is rare and mostly clean; ``nothing is selected'' in the proposal
should be read in this sense.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\mathrm{FDR}\ge\E[\mathrm{FDP}\,\1\{\text{selection}\}]\ge(1-\rho/\rho_{\mathrm{sel}})\Pr(\text{selection})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Per-class calibration]
Suppose hypotheses and calibration points are split into $K$ classes, class $c$ holding a share
$\pi_c$ of both, and BH is run per class at level $\alpha$. A selection of $k_c\ge1$ points in class
$c$ requires
\[
\frac{k_c}{N}\ \ge\ \frac{\pi_c}{\alpha(\pi_cm+1)}=\frac{1}{\alpha(m+1/\pi_c)} .
\]
Per-class calibration therefore lowers the floor, as a fraction of all hypotheses, by the factor
$(m+1)/(m+1/\pi_c)$; for $K$ equal classes this is $1-(K-1)/(m+K)$, e.g.\ a $0.4\%$ change for
$m=2000$, $K=10$. As a fraction of the class itself the floor is $1/(\alpha(\pi_cm+1))$, about $K$
times $\rho_{\mathrm{sel}}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:floor}(i) within class $c$, with $N$ replaced by $\pi_cN$ and $m$ by
$\pi_cm$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The proposal states that per-class calibration ``does not lower'' the floor. The corollary makes
this precise: it lowers it by a factor $1-O(K/m)$, negligible at the calibration sizes studied.
\end{remark}

\section{Proposition 3: the presence test}

For $1\le k\le m$, let $T^{(k)}_j$ be the number of points of $\D_j$ whose score is strictly larger
than the $k$-th largest calibration score, and
\[
\pi^{(k)}_j=F_{\mathrm{HG}}\bigl(k-1;\ N_j+m,\ m,\ T^{(k)}_j+k-1\bigr),
\]
where $F_{\mathrm{HG}}(x;M,K,d)$ is the distribution function of the number of marked items in $d$
draws without replacement from $M$ items of which $K$ are marked; $\pi_j^{(k)}=1$ when
$T^{(k)}_j=0$. For $k=1$, $\pi^{(1)}_j=\binom{N_j}{T_j}\big/\binom{N_j+m}{T_j}$ (proposal eq.~(4)).

\begin{proposition}[Prop.~3]\label{prop:presence}
Under A1 and A2, if fold $j$ and $\Cal$ contain no poisoned point, then
$\Pr(\pi^{(k)}_j\le u)\le u$ for all $u\in[0,1]$. Consequently the test that declares the dataset
attacked when $\min_j\pi^{(k)}_j\le\alpha/J$ has false-alarm probability at most $\alpha$ when no
fold and no calibration point is poisoned, whatever the dependence between folds.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Condition on $\G_j$. With no poison in $\D_j\cup\Cal$, $\Q_j=\D_j\cup\Cal$ and, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:structure}, the $N_j+m$ scores are fixed while the split into $N_j$ fold and $m$
calibration points is uniform. Assume first that the scores are distinct and rank them from the
top. $T^{(k)}_j\ge t$ holds iff the $t$-th highest fold score lies above the $k$-th highest
calibration score, i.e.\ iff at most $k-1$ calibration points lie among the top $t+k-1$ ranks. The
number of calibration points among those ranks is hypergeometric with parameters
$(N_j+m,\,m,\,t+k-1)$, so
\[
\Pr\bigl(T^{(k)}_j\ge t\mid\G_j\bigr)=F_{\mathrm{HG}}(k-1;N_j+m,m,t+k-1)=:G(t),
\]
which is non-increasing in $t$. Hence $\pi^{(k)}_j=G(T^{(k)}_j)$ is the upper-tail probability of
$T^{(k)}_j$ at its observed value, and for any $u$, with $t_u=\min\{t:G(t)\le u\}$,
$\Pr(\pi^{(k)}_j\le u)=\Pr(T^{(k)}_j\ge t_u)=G(t_u)\le u$. For $k=1$,
$G(t)=\binom{N_j}{t}/\binom{N_j+m}{t}$, the probability that the top $t$ ranks all belong to the
fold. Ties: Remark~\ref{rem:ties-pres}. The final claim is Bonferroni's inequality over the $J$
folds, which needs no assumption on their dependence.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Ties in Proposition 3]\label{rem:ties-pres}
Break ties by independent uniforms. The $k$-th calibration point in the broken order has the
$k$-th largest calibration value $v$, and every fold point with score strictly above $v$ is ranked
above it, so $T^{(k)}_j\le\tilde T^{(k)}_j$, the count after tie-breaking, whose law is the
hypergeometric one above. Since $G$ is non-increasing, $\pi^{(k)}_j\ge G(\tilde T^{(k)}_j)$ and the
test remains valid (conservative).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What a rejection says; the $k>1$ variant]
The null of Proposition~\ref{prop:presence} is ``no poison in $\D_j\cup\Cal$''. A rejection
therefore says that the dataset is attacked, not that fold $j$ is: poisoned calibration points
that score low make $\pi_j$ smaller, because they enlarge $m$ without changing the count. The
$k$-th-largest variant is valid under the same null for every $k$; its point is power: up to $k-1$
\emph{high}-scoring poisoned calibration points cannot hide poisoned fold points. Under
contamination the default ($k=1$) is blocked by a single such point (Study~S2, finding~(7)).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Shared calibration set]
All folds use the same $\Cal$, so the $\pi_j$ are dependent (positively, in simulation). The
Bonferroni combination is valid regardless; a combination that assumes independent folds (Fisher,
\v{S}id\'ak) would not be. Study~S2 measures the cost: false-alarm rate about $0.027$ at
$\alpha=0.05$.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Threshold.} For $k=1$,
$\binom{N}{t}\big/\binom{N+m}{t}=\prod_{u=0}^{t-1}\frac{N-u}{N+m-u}\le\bigl(\frac{N}{N+m}\bigr)^t$,
since each factor is at most $N/(N+m)$. The test therefore rejects whenever
\[
T_j\ \ge\ \frac{\ln(J/\alpha)}{\ln(1+m/N_j)},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
\rho_{\mathrm{pres}}\approx\frac{\ln(J/\alpha)}{m}\quad(N_j\gg m),
\]
counting poison that scores above every calibration point (\texttt{cord floors}; Table~5).
The ratio to the selection floor is $\rho_{\mathrm{pres}}/\rho_{\mathrm{sel}}\approx\alpha\ln(J/\alpha)$,
$0.18$ at $\alpha=0.05$, $J=2$.

\section{Proposition 4: contaminated calibration}

Let the calibration set left after the audit be $\Cal=C^0\cup B$, with $C^0$ clean,
$m^0=|C^0|$, $B$ poisoned and $\varepsilon=|B|/m$. Write $p^0_i=(1+V^0_j(s_j(i)))/(1+m^0)$ for the
p-value computed against $C^0$ alone, and $m_{\mathrm{pre}}$ for the calibration size before the
audit.

\begin{proposition}[Prop.~4]\label{prop:contamination}
Assume A1 and A2 for the clean points, the scores of the poisoned points being arbitrary.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item For every audit that removes only poisoned points and every clean $i\in\D_j$,
  $\Pr(p_i\le t)\le t\,(1+m_{\mathrm{pre}})/(1+m^0)$.
\end{enumerate}
If moreover $B$ is $\G_j$-measurable (no audit; an audit that removes every poisoned point; or any
audit whose decisions do not depend on the clean calibration points) and A3 holds, then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),resume]
\item fold-wise BH at level $\alpha$ has
  $\mathrm{FDR}\le\alpha\,\frac{\E[N_j^0]}{N_j}\cdot\frac{1+m}{1+m^0}\le\frac{\alpha}{1-\varepsilon}$;
\item $\sum_{i\in\D_j\cap\Hnull}\E[e_i]\le N_j^0\,(1+m)/(1+m^0)$, and e-BH on the pooled e-values has
  $\mathrm{FDR}\le\alpha/(1-\varepsilon_{\max})$, with $\varepsilon_{\max}$ the largest contaminated
  share over folds.
\end{enumerate}
These bounds hold whatever the poisoned calibration points score. The worst placement is below
every clean point, where the e-value bound in (c) is nearly attained; poison that scores high makes
both procedures conservative.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) $p_i=(1+V^0_j+V^B_j)/(1+m)\ge(1+V^0_j)/(1+m_{\mathrm{pre}})=p^0_i\,(1+m^0)/(1+m_{\mathrm{pre}})$,
since $V^B_j\ge0$ and $m\le m_{\mathrm{pre}}$. The audit removes only poisoned points, so $C^0$ is the
whole clean calibration part and $p^0_i$ is super-uniform by Proposition~1(i) applied to $C^0$.
Hence $\Pr(p_i\le t)\le\Pr\bigl(p^0_i\le t(1+m_{\mathrm{pre}})/(1+m^0)\bigr)\le t(1+m_{\mathrm{pre}})/(1+m^0)$.

(b) At the stopping value $T_\alpha$ the BH condition gives
$1/K_j\le\alpha(1+m)/\bigl(N_j(1+V_j)\bigr)\le\alpha(1+m)/\bigl(N_j(1+V^0_j)\bigr)$, evaluated at
$T_\alpha$. Hence $\mathrm{FDP}\le\frac{\alpha(1+m)}{N_j}\cdot K^0_j(T_\alpha)/(1+V^0_j(T_\alpha))$. With
$B\in\G_j$, the BH rule depends on $V_j=V^0_j+V^B_j$ with $V^B_j$ fixed, so $T_\alpha$ is a stopping
value and Lemma~\ref{lem:stopping} gives
$\E[\mathrm{FDP}\mid\G_j]\le\alpha N_j^0(1+m)/\bigl(N_j(1+m^0)\bigr)$. Finally
$(1+m)(1-\varepsilon)=1-\varepsilon+m^0\le1+m^0$.

(c) $\sum_{i\in\D_j\cap\Hnull}e_i=(1+m)K^0_j(T_j)/(1+V_j(T_j))\le(1+m)K^0_j(T_j)/(1+V^0_j(T_j))$;
Lemma~\ref{lem:stopping} gives the first claim, and the e-BH argument of Proposition~1(iii) the
second. Near-attainment in (c): when every poisoned calibration score lies below every clean
score, $V^B_j=0$ at every threshold the scan can stop at, so $V_j=V^0_j$ there, and the only
remaining slack is the optional-stopping inequality, which is an equality unless the scan reaches
$Y_k=0$ before stopping (Lemma~\ref{lem:martingale}). The FDR bound in (b) has further slack from
the BH condition $1/K_j\le\cdots$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is still open]
(i) The default audit inspects the top-$b$ calibration points \emph{by score}. Which poisoned
points it reaches then depends on how many clean calibration points score above them, so $B$ is
not $\G_j$-measurable and (b)--(c) are not covered; (a) is. Study~S2 finding~(5) observed FDR at
most $0.047$ at $\alpha=0.05$ with this audit. (ii) An audit that also removes clean points by score
breaks A1 itself; Study~S2 finding~(6) shows the FDR can then reach $0.31$ at $\alpha=0.05$, which is
why the audit rule removes points only on positive evidence of tampering.
\end{remark}
